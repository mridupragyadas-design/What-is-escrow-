How Escrow Works

A plain-language guide before you trade

The basics

Suppose you're buying an item for ₹10,000.

  1. Buyer and seller agree to the deal.
  2. The buyer sends ₹10,000 to the escrow service, not directly to the seller.
  3. The escrow service confirms that the money has been received.
  4. The seller sends the item to the buyer.
  5. If the buyer receives the correct item and everything is as agreed, the buyer confirms the deal.
  6. The escrow service then releases the money to the seller.

What if the item isn't received?

If the seller doesn't send the item, the buyer can report the problem to the escrow service. The money is still being held by escrow, so the seller shouldn't automatically get it just because the buyer paid. The escrow service can investigate the situation and, depending on the agreed rules and evidence, refund the buyer or resolve the dispute.

What if the buyer claims they didn't receive it, but the seller says they did?

That's where proof and the escrow terms matter. Tracking, delivery confirmation, communication, and other evidence may be considered.

Escrow doesn't automatically mean the buyer always wins. The purpose is to hold the funds safely until the agreed conditions are met, and to provide a process for disputes.

In simple terms

Without escrow:
Buyer → 💰 → Seller → 📦 → Buyer
If something goes wrong, getting the money back can be difficult.
With escrow:
Buyer → 💰 → 🛡️ Escrow → 💰 → Seller
Seller → 📦 → Buyer
If something goes wrong, the money can remain held while the issue is resolved.

Important: a person simply calling themselves a "middleman" doesn't make them legitimate escrow. Both parties should agree beforehand on who controls the funds, the release conditions, dispute rules, fees, and what evidence is required.
